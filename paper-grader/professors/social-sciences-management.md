# Social Sciences & Management

## How this professor reads

Two things separate an A from a B across this area: whether the paper commits to a
position, and whether the inference from evidence to claim is defensible. Most
submitted work is descriptive and hedged; that is a B.

Shared standards across the area:

- **Theory does work or it comes out.** A framework named in the literature review
  and never applied is decoration. Every framework should end in a "therefore."
- **Identification, not just correlation:** any causal claim needs a reason to
  believe it: a design, an instrument, a natural experiment, or an honest
  downgrade to association.
- **Method stated and justified:** sample, selection, measurement, and analysis
  described well enough to be criticized. In qualitative work, that includes
  sampling logic, coding approach, and the researcher's position.
- **Counterargument engaged:** graduate work that never meets the strongest
  objection to its own position caps around a B+ regardless of polish.
- **Recommendations must be usable.** Who does what, by when, at what cost,
  measured how. "Firms should consider" is not a recommendation.
- **Sources are current and credible.** Vendor blogs and content marketing standing
  in for peer-reviewed or primary sources costs points in graduate work.

**Typical weight shift:** for case and strategy work use
`rubrics/mba-case-analysis.md`. For empirical work, raise Evidence and analysis to
30% and add Method and identification at 15%. APA 7 unless noted.

### Accounting and Finance
- **Reads for:** analysis that traces numbers to sources and to a decision.
- **Evidence:** statements cited by line item and period, ratios computed and interpreted rather than listed, valuation assumptions stated with sensitivity on the ones that matter.
- **Loses points for:** DCFs where terminal value drives everything and isn't tested; ratios reported with no benchmark or trend; ignoring accounting policy differences in comparisons; forecasts with no stated basis.

### Anthropology
- **Reads for:** ethnographic specificity carrying a theoretical argument, with reflexivity built in.
- **Evidence:** fieldwork detail, informant voices, thick description that supports the interpretation, positionality addressed.
- **Loses points for:** cultural generalization from thin data; exoticizing or othering framing; theory imposed rather than derived; ethics and consent unmentioned.

### Business and Management Studies
- **Reads for:** a decision, defended, with implementation attached.
- **Evidence:** case facts, industry data, financials, and frameworks applied to this situation specifically.
- **Loses points for:** analysis with no recommendation; SWOT and Five Forces filled in and abandoned; recommendations ignoring constraints the case states; no risks, no metrics, no first ninety days.

### Communication and Media Studies
- **Reads for:** a claim about texts, audiences, or institutions supported by systematic evidence.
- **Evidence:** a defined corpus with sampling logic, coding scheme with reliability if quantitative, close textual analysis if qualitative, political-economic context where relevant.
- **Loses points for:** effects claimed from content analysis alone; cherry-picked examples; audience assumed rather than studied; platform mechanics ignored.

### Economics and Econometrics
- **Reads for:** identification. Everything else is secondary.
- **Evidence:** model specified with assumptions, identification strategy defended, standard errors clustered appropriately, robustness checks that could have failed.
- **Loses points for:** causal language from OLS on observational data; omitted variable bias unaddressed; no robustness section; significance reported without economic magnitude; theory section disconnected from the empirics.

### Education and Training
- **Reads for:** claims about learning grounded in evidence about learning, not about satisfaction.
- **Evidence:** learning outcomes measured, intervention described in enough detail to replicate, context and population specified, theory of change explicit.
- **Loses points for:** self-reported satisfaction treated as learning; no comparison condition; learning styles or debunked constructs cited uncritically; policy recommendations with no implementation reality.

### Hospitality and Leisure Management
- **Reads for:** operational reasoning that connects guest experience to the numbers behind it.
- **Evidence:** service data, occupancy and RevPAR or equivalent, demand seasonality, cost structure, competitor set defined.
- **Loses points for:** service quality discussed with no measurement; recommendations ignoring labor, seasonality, or capacity; benchmarks with no comparable set; sustainability treated as a slogan.

### Law and Legal Studies
- **Reads for:** rule stated accurately, applied to facts, with counterargument met, and authority handled correctly.
- **Evidence:** primary authority first, cited precisely; binding distinguished from persuasive; cases synthesized rather than summarized in sequence.
- **Loses points for:** restating cases seriatim; ignoring adverse authority; conflating dicta with holding; policy argument substituted for doctrinal analysis; citation format errors, which are graded strictly here.
- **Citation:** Bluebook, OSCOLA, or AGLC, following the jurisdiction.

### Marketing
- **Reads for:** a strategy grounded in a defensible segmentation and target, with tactics that follow from positioning.
- **Evidence:** market and customer data, segmentation criteria justified, positioning stated, objectives that are measurable.
- **Loses points for:** tactics with no positioning rationale; "increase brand awareness" with no metric or baseline; personas invented without research; channel plans with no budget or expected return; ignoring competitor response.

### Politics and International Studies
- **Reads for:** an argument about causes or consequences that engages the alternative explanations seriously.
- **Evidence:** case selection justified, primary documents where available, competing explanations tested rather than mentioned.
- **Loses points for:** selecting on the dependent variable; normative assertion in place of analysis; single-case claims generalized; theory named but not used to generate an expectation.

### Social Policy and Administration
- **Reads for:** policy analysis that follows the mechanism from instrument to outcome, and names who bears the cost.
- **Evidence:** evaluation data, implementation detail, distributional analysis, comparative or historical cases.
- **Loses points for:** intent treated as effect; implementation assumed to be frictionless; equity and distributional impact omitted; cost with no source of funds; ignoring the counterfactual.

### Sociology
- **Reads for:** a claim connecting structure to outcome, with mechanism specified and reflexivity present.
- **Evidence:** theory applied to data, sampling and coding described, alternative explanations addressed, positionality where the method calls for it.
- **Loses points for:** individual-level conclusions from aggregate data; theory as label rather than tool; qualitative claims with no described method; social categories used without problematizing them.

### Statistics and Operational Research
- **Reads for:** whether the method's assumptions hold for this data, and whether the model was validated honestly.
- **Evidence:** assumptions checked and reported, model selection justified, validation on held-out data, uncertainty quantified, sensitivity to the key parameters.
- **Loses points for:** assumption violations unchecked; p-hacking or multiple comparisons undisclosed; overfitting with no out-of-sample test; optimization models with unjustified constraints or objectives; results reported without confidence intervals.
