# Life Sciences & Medicine

## How this professor reads

The question is always what the evidence can actually support. Overclaiming is the
cardinal sin, and in clinical subjects it is a safety issue as well as an academic one.

Shared standards across the area:

- **Match the claim to the study design.** Observational data supports association,
  not causation. Animal or in-vitro results do not transfer to humans without saying
  so. A causal verb attached to a correlational finding is a serious finding.
- **Evidence hierarchy is real.** Systematic reviews and RCTs outrank cohort studies,
  which outrank case reports, which outrank expert opinion. Cite the best available
  and say when it's weak.
- **Sample size, power, and controls:** underpowered studies and missing controls get
  named. So does a negative result reported as proof of no effect.
- **Statistics interpreted, not just reported:** a p-value with no effect size and no
  confidence interval tells the reader almost nothing.
- **Limitations section is mandatory** and must name limitations that
  threaten the conclusion, not decorative ones.
- **Ethics:** approval, consent, welfare, and conflicts of interest stated where the
  work involves humans or animals.
- **Currency:** in fast-moving clinical fields, sources older than five years need a
  reason to be there.

**Typical weight shift** from `rubrics/default-academic.md`: raise Evidence and
analysis to 35%, add Method and rigor at 15%, cut Thesis to 10%. APA 7 or Vancouver
depending on subject.

### Agriculture and Forestry
- **Reads for:** field-scale reasoning that accounts for site variability, season, and management practice.
- **Evidence:** trial design with replication and blocking, soil and climate data, yield or growth measured with variance reported.
- **Loses points for:** single-site single-season results generalized broadly; no control plot; economics of the practice ignored; environmental externalities left out.

### Anatomy and Physiology
- **Reads for:** correct structural detail tied to mechanism and function.
- **Evidence:** standard anatomical terminology and planes, mechanisms traced through the relevant pathway, clinical correlation where the course expects it.
- **Loses points for:** naming structures without relating them to function; vague or lay terminology; imprecise relationships; ignoring variation and its clinical significance.

### Biological Sciences
- **Reads for:** a testable hypothesis, appropriate controls, and interpretation that stops where the data stops.
- **Evidence:** methods reproducible in detail, n stated, statistical tests appropriate to the data and justified, figures with error bars defined in the caption.
- **Loses points for:** no negative or vehicle control; pseudoreplication; representative images with no quantification; discussion that drifts into speculation without marking it as such.

### Dentistry
- **Reads for:** clinical reasoning from findings to diagnosis to a defensible treatment plan.
- **Evidence:** examination findings, radiographic interpretation, current clinical guidelines cited, alternatives considered.
- **Loses points for:** treatment plan with no differential or no alternatives; ignoring patient factors, cost, or maintenance; guidelines cited without their evidence grade; radiographic claims unsupported by what's visible.

### Medicine
- **Reads for:** clinical reasoning that is explicit and safe, with a differential that was worked through rather than gestured at.
- **Evidence:** guidelines and primary literature with evidence levels; investigations justified; red flags addressed.
- **Loses points for:** anchoring on one diagnosis; a differential with no exclusion reasoning; dosing or management advice without a source; failing to state uncertainty; ignoring comorbidity and contraindications.
- **Note:** this skill grades academic writing. It does not verify clinical accuracy for patient use, and the report should say so where the paper contains management advice.

### Nursing
- **Reads for:** evidence-based practice applied to a patient situation, with the care rationale made explicit.
- **Evidence:** current guidelines and primary research; frameworks such as the nursing process or a reflective model applied properly, not just named.
- **Loses points for:** interventions listed without rationale; reflection that describes without analyzing; patient safety, dignity, or consent unaddressed; scope-of-practice boundaries crossed.

### Pharmacy and Pharmacology
- **Reads for:** mechanism, kinetics, and clinical consequence connected end to end.
- **Evidence:** receptor and pathway detail, PK/PD parameters with sources, dose–response data, interaction and contraindication evidence.
- **Loses points for:** mechanism described without therapeutic consequence; dosing without population adjustment for renal, hepatic, or age factors; interactions omitted; adverse effects listed without frequency or severity.

### Psychology
- **Reads for:** a hypothesis derived from theory, an appropriate design, and interpretation that respects what the design can support.
- **Evidence:** sample described fully, measures with reliability and validity evidence, effect sizes and confidence intervals alongside p-values, power addressed.
- **Loses points for:** causal language from correlational designs; HARKing or undisclosed multiple comparisons; convenience samples generalized broadly; construct validity assumed; ethics approval unmentioned.

### Veterinary Science
- **Reads for:** species-appropriate clinical reasoning, with welfare central rather than appended.
- **Evidence:** species-specific physiology and pharmacology, evidence-based protocols, differential worked through.
- **Loses points for:** extrapolating across species without justification; drug doses not species-adjusted; welfare and analgesia treated as secondary; owner factors, cost, and zoonotic risk ignored.
