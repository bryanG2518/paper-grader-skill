# Natural Sciences

## How this professor reads

Quantitative honesty is the standard. A number without an uncertainty is not a
result, and a conclusion that outruns its error bars is wrong regardless of how well
it's written.

Shared standards across the area:

- **Uncertainty is not optional.** Every measured or derived quantity carries an
  uncertainty with a stated basis. Propagate it. A result quoted to more precision
  than the uncertainty allows is an error, not a style choice.
- **Units and significant figures, consistently:** dimensional analysis is a check
  the professor will run on any unfamiliar equation.
- **Assumptions and approximations:** small-angle, ideal, adiabatic,
  steady-state, named where used and justified.
- **Derivations shown or properly referenced:** a result pulled from nowhere is
  unmarkable even when correct.
- **Figures are arguments.** Axes labeled with units, error bars defined, fits
  justified rather than assumed, captions that stand alone.
- **Anomalies are discussed, not smoothed.** A data point that doesn't fit is the
  most interesting thing in the paper, and hiding it is worse than not explaining it.
- **Discussion separates what was measured from what is inferred.**

**Typical weight shift** from `rubrics/default-academic.md`: raise Evidence and
analysis to 35%, add Method and uncertainty at 15%, cut Thesis to 10%.

### Chemistry
- **Reads for:** mechanism and quantitative rigor, with structures and conditions specified exactly.
- **Evidence:** balanced equations, stoichiometry, yields with limiting reagent identified, spectra assigned peak by peak, characterization sufficient to support the structural claim.
- **Loses points for:** mechanisms with unbalanced or unexplained arrow pushing; purity claimed without characterization; yields with no basis; hazard and waste handling omitted from an experimental section.

### Earth and Marine Sciences
- **Reads for:** interpretation constrained by sampling reality: where the data came from, and what it can represent.
- **Evidence:** sample locations and depths, dating or proxy methods with their errors, oceanographic or sedimentary context, temporal resolution stated.
- **Loses points for:** proxies interpreted without their calibration limits; spatial or seasonal aliasing ignored; correlation between records treated as causation; scale mismatch between data and claim.

### Environmental Sciences
- **Reads for:** systems reasoning that carries uncertainty through to the conclusion.
- **Evidence:** monitoring data with methods and detection limits, model assumptions stated, baselines and controls defined, scale made explicit.
- **Loses points for:** advocacy replacing analysis; trends claimed from short records; confounders unaddressed; policy recommendations with no feasibility or cost consideration; single-medium analysis presented as whole-system.

### Geography
- **Reads for:** attention to scale, place, and process: spatial patterns explained rather than mapped.
- **Evidence:** data with projection and resolution stated, maps that answer a question, and, in human geography, positionality and method acknowledged.
- **Loses points for:** maps with no scale, projection, or legend; ecological fallacy; MAUP ignored; description of a pattern with no explanatory mechanism; qualitative claims with no method.

### Geology
- **Reads for:** reasoning from field and petrographic evidence to earth history, with alternatives considered.
- **Evidence:** stratigraphic and structural observations, sample descriptions, cross-sections that balance, geochronology with uncertainties.
- **Loses points for:** interpretation presented as observation; cross-sections that don't balance; a single hypothesis with no alternatives tested; regional claims from one outcrop.

### Geophysics
- **Reads for:** awareness that the inversion is non-unique and the resolution is finite.
- **Evidence:** acquisition parameters, processing sequence, inversion regularization stated, resolution and sensitivity tests, uncertainty on the model.
- **Loses points for:** models presented as the answer rather than an answer; no resolution or checkerboard test; processing choices unreported; interpreting structure below the resolvable scale.

### Materials Sciences
- **Reads for:** the processing–structure–property–performance chain connected all the way through.
- **Evidence:** processing history stated, characterization matched to the length scale of the claim, mechanical or functional testing with sample counts and scatter.
- **Loses points for:** structure claimed from one technique where confirmation was needed; property data from n=1; micrographs with no scale bar; failure analysis with no fractography.

### Mathematics
- **Reads for:** whether the proof is actually a proof.
- **Evidence:** hypotheses stated, every step justified, edge and degenerate cases handled, quantifiers used precisely, counterexamples given where a claim fails.
- **Loses points for:** "clearly" or "it follows" standing in for a step; circular reasoning; proving the converse; unstated assumptions about existence, convergence, or well-definedness; notation introduced without definition.

### Physics and Astronomy
- **Reads for:** physical reasoning, with results checked against limits and orders of magnitude.
- **Evidence:** derivations from stated principles, uncertainties propagated, systematic errors considered separately from statistical, comparison with theory or prior measurement.
- **Loses points for:** systematics ignored; results quoted without uncertainty; fits without goodness-of-fit; failure to check limiting cases; discrepancy with accepted values noted but not investigated.
