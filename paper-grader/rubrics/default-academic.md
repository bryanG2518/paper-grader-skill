# Default academic rubric

Use when no professor rubric was supplied and the paper is a research paper, term
paper, or analytical essay. Tell the user this is the rubric you applied.

| Category | Weight | What full marks look like |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt alignment | 20% | Addresses every task the assignment names, at the depth it asks for, with nothing substantial out of scope |
| Thesis and argument | 20% | One arguable claim, stated early, carried through every section, and defended rather than restated |
| Evidence and analysis | 25% | Claims backed by credible sources or data; the paper interprets the evidence rather than reporting it |
| Structure and flow | 15% | Sections build on each other; each paragraph has one job; transitions carry the argument forward |
| Sources and citation | 10% | Appropriate number, quality, and recency of sources; in-text citations match the reference list |
| Mechanics and format | 10% | Grammar, syntax, page/word count, headings, and required formatting all clean |

## Scoring each category

Score 0–100 within the category, then weight it.

- **90–100** — Nothing a grader would mark down. Would be used as a model answer.
- **80–89** — Solid and complete, but one identifiable weakness: a thin section, an unexamined assumption, a claim carried by assertion.
- **70–79** — Meets the requirement in name. Describes where it should analyze, or covers the topic without committing to a position.
- **60–69** — A component the assignment required is missing or badly underdeveloped.
- **Below 60** — Does not do what was asked.

## Common point losses, in order of how often they appear

1. **Describing instead of analyzing:** the paper explains what happened but never says what it means or why it matters. Test: delete every sentence that only reports a fact. If little remains, this is the finding.
2. **Thesis drift:** the introduction promises one argument, the body delivers another. Check the conclusion against the intro; if they'd suit different papers, flag it.
3. **Unsupported analytic claims:** any sentence carrying the argument that has no citation, no data, and no reasoning chain.
4. **No counterargument:** graduate work that never acknowledges the strongest objection to its own position caps out around a B+, regardless of polish.
5. **Source quality:** vendor blogs, content-marketing pages, and undated web sources substituting for peer-reviewed literature, primary documents, or industry data.
6. **Uneven depth:** one section is three pages of analysis, another is two paragraphs, usually a sign of a section written last, at speed.
7. **A conclusion that only summarizes:** it should state what follows from the argument, not repeat the section headings.
